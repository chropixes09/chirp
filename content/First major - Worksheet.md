#### **Question 1**

**(a)** $S_\infty = \frac{u_1}{1-r} \implies \frac{27}{1-r} = \frac{81}{2} \implies 54 = 81(1-r) \implies 1-r = \frac{2}{3} \implies r = \frac{1}{3}$.

**(b)** $u_2 = 27 \left(\frac{1}{3}\right) = 9$, $u_4 = 27 \left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^3 = 1$.
For arithmetic sequence $v_n$: $v_2 = 9$, $v_4 = 1$.
Common difference $d = \frac{v_4 - v_2}{2} = \frac{1 - 9}{2} = -4$.
First term $v_1 = v_2 - d = 9 - (-4) = 13$.
Sum $\sum_{n=1}^N v_n = \frac{N}{2}[2(13) + (N-1)(-4)] = \frac{N}{2}(30 - 4N) = N(15 - 2N) > 0$.
Since $N > 0$, we require $15 - 2N > 0 \implies N < 7.5$.
The greatest integer $N$ is $7$.

#### **Question 2**

**(a)** The terms $A_3 = a+2d$, $A_4 = a+3d$, and $A_7 = a+6d$ form a geometric sequence:

$$
\frac{a+3d}{a+2d} = \frac{a+6d}{a+3d} \implies (a+3d)^2 = (a+2d)(a+6d)
$$

$$
a^2 + 6ad + 9d^2 = a^2 + 8ad + 12d^2 \implies -2ad = 3d^2
$$


Since $d \ne 0$, divide by $d$: $a = -\frac{3}{2}d$.

**(b)** First term of GP: $G_1 = a+2d = -\frac{3}{2}d + 2d = \frac{1}{2}d$.
Second term of GP: $G_2 = a+3d = \frac{3}{2}d$.
Common ratio $r = \frac{G_2}{G_1} = 3$.
$4^{\text{th}}$ term of GP: $G_4 = G_1 \cdot r^3 = \frac{1}{2}d (3^3) = \frac{27}{2}d$.
$16^{\text{th}}$ term of AP: $A_{16} = a + 15d = -\frac{3}{2}d + 15d = \frac{27}{2}d$.
Thus, $G_4 = A_{16}$.

### SECTION B: Binomial Theorem

#### **Question 1**

**(a)** $\binom{3n+1}{3n-2} = \binom{3n+1}{3} = \frac{(3n+1)(3n)(3n-1)}{3 \times 2 \times 1} =$ $\frac{9n^3 - n}{2}$.
**(b)** $\frac{9n^3 - n}{2} > 10^6 \implies 9n^3 - n > 2 \times 10^6$.
For $n=60$: $9(216000) - 60 = 1\,943\,940$.
For $n=61$: $9(226981) - 61 = 2\,042\,768 > 2 \times 10^6$. Least value is $n = 61$.

#### **Question 2**

In $\left(\frac{1}{x} + 5x\right)^8$, general term $T_{r+1} = \binom{8}{r} x^{-(8-r)} (5x)^r = \binom{8}{r} 5^r x^{2r-8}$.
For $x^2 \implies 2r - 8 = 2 \implies r = 5$. Coefficient $= \binom{8}{5} 5^5 = 175\,000$.
In $(a+5x)^7$, $x^4$ term is $\binom{7}{4} a^3 (5x)^4 = 21875 a^3 x^4$.
$21875 a^3 = 175000 \implies a^3 = 8 \implies$ $a = 2$.

#### **Question 3**

General term in $\left(4x^2 - \frac{3}{2x}\right)^{12}$ is $T_{r+1} = \binom{12}{r} (4x^2)^{12-r} \left(-\frac{3}{2x}\right)^r = \binom{12}{r} 4^{12-r} \left(-\frac{3}{2}\right)^r x^{24-3r}$.
For constant term, $24 - 3r = 0 \implies r = 8$.
$T_9 = \binom{12}{8} 4^4 \left(-\frac{3}{2}\right)^8 = 495 \times 256 \times \frac{6561}{256} =$ $3\,247\,695$.

#### **Question 4**

Terms in second expansion: $x^{-6} (r=0), 12x^{-4} (r=1), 60x^{-2} (r=2), 160 (r=3), 240x^2 (r=4)$.
To obtain $x^{-2}$ when multiplied by $(x^3 - 3x^2 + 3x - 1)$:

* $(-3x^2) \times (12x^{-4}) = -36x^{-2}$

* $(-1) \times (60x^{-2}) = -60x^{-2}$
  Total coefficient $= -36 - 60 =$ $-96$.

#### **Question 5**

$\left(x - \frac{2}{x}\right)^4 = \sum_{r=0}^4 \binom{4}{r} (-2)^r x^{4-2r}$.
$\left(x^2 + \frac{2}{x}\right)^3 = \sum_{k=0}^3 \binom{3}{k} 2^k x^{6-3k}$.
We need powers to cancel: $(4-2r) + (6-3k) = 0 \implies 2r + 3k = 10$.
With $0 \le r \le 4$ and $0 \le k \le 3$, the only valid solution is $k=2, r=2$.
For $r=2$: $\binom{4}{2}(-2)^2 = 24$. For $k=2$: $\binom{3}{2}(2)^2 = 12$.
Constant term $= 24 \times 12 =$ $288$.

### SECTION C: Exponents and Logarithms

#### **Question 1**

Let $u = \ln x$. $u^2 - (\ln 2)u - 2(\ln 2)^2 < 0 \implies (u - 2\ln 2)(u + \ln 2) < 0$.
$-\ln 2 < \ln x < 2\ln 2 \implies \ln(1/2) < \ln x < \ln 4 \implies$ $\frac{1}{2} < x < 4$.

#### **Question 2**

$\log_2(x/5) = \log_2 4 + \log_2 3 = \log_2 12 \implies x/5 = 12 \implies$ $x = 60$.

#### **Question 3**

$\log_2[(x+3)(x-3)] = 4 \implies x^2 - 9 = 2^4 = 16 \implies x^2 = 25 \implies$ $x = 5$ (rejecting $x = -5$ since $x > 3$).

#### **Question 4**

$(2^x)^2 + 4(2^x) - 3 = 0$. Let $y = 2^x > 0$.
$y = \frac{-4 + \sqrt{16 + 12}}{2} = \sqrt{7} - 2$.
$2^x = \sqrt{7} - 2 \implies$ $x = \log_2(\sqrt{7} - 2)$ or $\frac{\ln(\sqrt{7} - 2)}{\ln 2}$.

#### **Question 5**

$10 \log_9 6 = 10 \cdot \frac{\log_3 6}{\log_3 9} = 5 \log_3(2 \times 3) = 5 + 5\log_3 2$.
$m - n\log_3 2 = 5 + 5\log_3 2 \implies$ $m = 5, n = -5$.

#### **Question 6**

**(a)** $a \in (0, 1) \cup (1, \infty)$ (or $a > 0, a \ne 1$).
**(b)** $\log_x y = \frac{4}{\log_x y} \implies (\log_x y)^2 = 4 \implies \log_x y = \pm 2$.
Expressions: $y = x^2$ or $y = \frac{1}{x^2}$ (for $x > 0, x \ne 1$).

#### **Question 7**

$a = \frac{\ln 3}{\ln 2} \cdot \frac{\ln 4}{\ln 3} \dots \frac{\ln 32}{\ln 31} = \frac{\ln 32}{\ln 2} = \log_2 32 =$ $5$.
